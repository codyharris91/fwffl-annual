Part One

The Three Doors

The unit here is the started point — points a player actually put into a lineup for the team that acquired him. Bench points are not value delivered, and a brilliant pickup nobody starts helped nobody. Credit follows the player: draft a receiver, trade him in week 6, and the draft keeps weeks 1–5 while the trade takes what comes after.

Part Four

What Actually Decides It

Two questions get confused with each other. How much of a roster comes from the draft is one thing — and the answer barely separates champions from also-rans. How good the draft was is another, and it separates them almost completely.

Drafting Well Is Close to Decisive

Every manager-season sorted by the quality of its picks — points above or below what each pick's position normally returns at that slot — then split into thirds.

Draft qualitySeasonsMade playoffs TitlesAverage finish
Drafted best 19 84.2% 4 3.7
Drafted middling 18 55.6% 1 5.5
Drafted worst 17 11.8% 0 9.1

The bottom third has never produced a champion across 5 seasons, and reaches the playoffs about once in eight tries. Drafting badly is not a hole you usually climb out of.

Champions take 68.7% from the draft; teams that missed take 66.6%. The gap is nothing. What separates them is 483 points of output — not a different recipe, a better version of the same one. The one real difference in shape: losing teams lean hardest on the wire, because churning it is what you do when the roster is not working.

Ranking managers by points-from-their-own-picks would call all of these bad drafts. They were the opposite: good drafts, converted. Any honest reading of "can you overcome a bad draft" has to tell the two apart, which is why the tables above rank on pick quality rather than on what a manager kept.

Method

Acquisitions are replayed in order for every season, so each of 16048 rostered player-weeks carries the channel it arrived through. Credit follows the player: re-signing someone you cut starts a fresh attribution, so nobody keeps draft credit for a player they let go. Waiver claims and plain free-agent adds count as one channel — both are players the whole league could have had.

Values are started points only. Draft quality is measured against what each pick's position normally returns at that slot, which is what makes a seventeenth-round quarterback comparable to a first-round back.
